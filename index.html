What happened

This happened to me.

I run my side projects out of a free Jira site. Since June it had collected 100+ tickets: plans, decisions, half-finished ideas.

On September 20 I found the site returned a 404. Not "access denied". The whole site was gone. Support told me the data had already been deleted and couldn't be recovered.

I'd been using that Jira for months, almost daily through the API, from AI tools. But the activity Atlassian's docs list for inactivity is things a person does in the browser: opening a dashboard, viewing an issue. My guess is that API calls didn't count. The docs don't say that, so it's only a guess.

Here's the part that stung: Atlassian did warn me. Two warning emails in the two weeks before, the first one with the exact date. They landed in the same inbox as Jira's weekly digest emails, and I missed both.

Then I went looking at the other free tiers I use, and each one has its own rule. So I'm building the thing I wish I'd had: one place that keeps track of those deadlines and emails me before they arrive, with the warning emails collected in one spot.

The fine print

You're probably on at least one of these.

The rules below are from each provider's own docs (checked October 2026), except the Jira data deletion: that is what happened to me, not something the docs state. Rules change, which is part of the problem.

How it will work (in development)

How it will work.

01

Add a service

Pick it from the list, or forward its welcome or warning email to your private Keepalive address. Keepalive keeps each provider's rules, so you don't have to look them up. For services that don't publish a number of days, it works from the warning emails you forward.

02

It keeps the clock

Keepalive tracks the deadline for each project: an inactivity window counted from when you added it or last clicked "I checked in", or a fixed expiry date counted from creation. It's designed so you don't have to hand over passwords or API keys.

03

You get a heads-up

An email ahead of the deadline, so you have time to log in and do something. Click "I checked in" and the timer resets.

Fair question

"I missed an email. Why would another email help?"

Jira's warnings came from the same sender, looking the same as the weekly digest I'd learned to ignore. Keepalive is meant to email you when a deadline is close. I don't plan to send weekly digests.

"Can't I just set up a cron job?"

For Supabase, often yes. But a cron ping didn't save my Jira site: I was using it almost daily through the API and it was deactivated anyway. And it can't help when the expiry is fixed no matter what (Render's free Postgres). And the cron itself can be switched off: in a public repo, scheduled workflows are disabled after 60 days without repo activity.

A calendar reminder works for one service. Once you have a handful of free tiers, each has different and sometimes unpublished rules. Keepalive is meant to be the one place that keeps track of them. It doesn't fake activity on your accounts. It tells you, and you decide.
